## The challenge

Aucctus builds an AI operating system for enterprise innovation: agentic
engines that take an innovation team from a raw idea through market and
customer intelligence, testing, financial modelling and portfolio
prioritisation. Weeks of research in minutes. The product is real and the
team behind it has a decade of Fortune 500 innovation practice.

What it didn't have when I joined was a top of funnel. Innovation leaders
inside large companies are hard to reach, harder to get in a room, and every
one of them has already been pitched a "generic AI wrapper" this year. My
mandate is narrow: fill the funnel, through outreach, events and whatever
creative means work.

## What I do

**An events motion, from nothing.** We host an event every few weeks:
dinners, workshops, breakfasts. Small rooms, innovation leaders, a demo and
a conversation instead of a deck. Around those we work the largest
innovation conferences in the world, InnoLead and Innov8rs among them,
running booths and the after-events where the real meetings happen.

**Demo calls.** Every first meeting is built around one question: what are
you being asked to prove this year, and what's slowing you down? The answer
decides which parts of the product we show and which we leave out.

**A go-to-market system, built from scratch.** There was no CRM worth
inheriting, so I built our own: events, marketing, pipeline management and
the follow-ups between them in one place, with AI agents doing the drafting
and the remembering. It's what lets one person run an enterprise pipeline
without dropping threads.

## What's happened so far

In the first six months the funnel went from empty to a full corporate
pipeline, sourced almost entirely through the events and the system above.
The conferences turn into booked meetings rather than badge scans, and the
hosted events are the most reliable way to get a buyer into a real
conversation.

## What I'm still learning

The lessons so far are all logistical, and all avoidable: material and
equipment missing on the day, events timed badly against the buyers'
calendars, and invites that go out too late. Every one of those now lives
as a checklist in the system, so the next event doesn't repeat them.

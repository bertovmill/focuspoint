## The challenge

Aucctus builds an AI operating system for enterprise innovation: agentic
engines that take an innovation team from a raw idea through market and
customer intelligence, testing, financial modelling and portfolio
prioritisation. Weeks of research in minutes. The product was real and the
team behind it had a decade of Fortune 500 innovation practice.

What it didn't have was a top of funnel. Innovation leaders inside large
companies are hard to reach, harder to get in a room, and every one of them
has already been pitched a "generic AI wrapper" this year. My mandate when I
joined was narrow: fill the funnel, through outreach, events and whatever
creative means worked.

## What I did

**An events motion, from nothing.** We hosted an event every few weeks:
dinners, workshops, breakfasts. Small rooms, innovation leaders, a demo and
a conversation instead of a deck. Around those we worked the largest
innovation conferences in the world, InnoLead and Innov8rs among them,
running booths and the after-events where the real meetings happen.

**Demo calls.** Every first meeting was built around one question: what are
you being asked to prove this year, and what's slowing you down? The answer
decided which parts of the product we showed and which we left out.

**A go-to-market system, built from scratch.** There was no CRM worth
inheriting, so I built our own: events, marketing, pipeline management and
the follow-ups between them in one place, with AI agents doing the drafting
and the remembering. It's what lets one person run an enterprise pipeline
without dropping threads.

## What happened

In six months the funnel went from empty to a full corporate pipeline,
sourced almost entirely through the events and the system above. The
conferences turned into booked meetings rather than badge scans, and the
hosted events became the most reliable way to get a buyer into a real
conversation.

## What I'd do differently

The lessons were all logistical, and all avoidable: material and equipment
missing on the day, events timed badly against the buyers' calendars, and
invites that went out too late. Every one of those now lives as a checklist
in the system.

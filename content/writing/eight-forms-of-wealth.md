For a long time the only number I could tell you about my own life was a bank balance. Not because it was the most important one — because it was the only one anything was counting.

That's the trap. The thing that gets measured gets managed, and if only one thing is measured, only one thing gets managed. Everything else drifts, quietly, for years, and you don't notice until something breaks.

So I picked eight.

## The eight

- **Growth** — what I'm learning. Measured in books finished.
- **Wellness** — what I'm doing with my body. Measured in hours in the gym.
- **Family** — the people I'd drop anything for. Measured in memories captured.
- **Craft** — the work itself, done well. Measured in what I write down about it.
- **Money** — freedom to choose. Measured against a savings target.
- **Community** — the people I bring together. Measured in subscribers to the things I run.
- **Adventure** — the life you actually remember. Measured in trips taken.
- **Service** — what I give back. Measured in thank-yous sent.

None of these are perfect proxies. "Books finished" isn't the same as learning, and "trips taken" isn't the same as adventure. But a rough number you look at every day beats a perfect one you never compute.

## Why an agent keeps the scoreboard

Here's what I learned the first three times I tried this with a spreadsheet: I stopped updating the columns that were going badly.

That's not a discipline problem, it's a design problem. Any tracking system where *you* do the entering will slowly become a record of the areas where you're already winning.

So the scoreboard doesn't live with me. It lives with Cael, the agent I built. I mention in passing that I finished a book and it logs it. I say I did two hours at the gym and it logs it. I never open a form, never pick a category, never decide whether this one counts. The friction that used to let me look away is gone.

And then it looks back at me. Every morning it texts me what matters today. Every night it reads the last month of what I've captured and tells me the patterns I'd never spot myself.

## Publishing them

The numbers are [on this site](/building), live, straight from that database.

Not as a flex — several of them are unflattering, and the point is that they stay visible when they are. A goal you've told people about is much harder to quietly abandon than one living in a private note. Publishing is the last piece of the same idea: make it hard to stop looking.

The dollar figure behind Money stays private. Everything else, you can watch.

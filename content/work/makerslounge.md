## The challenge

Builders thrive when they share their work. The people building with AI in
Toronto had no shortage of ideas; what they lacked was a place to make those
ideas real by telling one person, face to face, and getting a reaction. Online
you post into a void. In a room, someone asks the question that changes the
project.

## What I did

I ran it the unglamorous way. Found venues. Invited people, one at a time at
first. Built the itinerary for each night so a first-timer knew what would
happen and a regular had something new. Bought the signage. Ordered the pizza.
Ran the event, then did it again a few weeks later.

The format stayed simple on purpose: show what you're building, meet the
people who care about the same problem, leave with one new person to follow
up with.

## What happened

MakersLounge is now a community of 1,500 builders in Toronto, and the thing
it produces is connections: hundreds of them, the kind that turn into
co-founders, hires, customers and friends. The most common thing people say
about it is that it's a very welcoming environment, which is the whole
point. A room where a beginner can show a half-finished thing without
flinching is a room where the good ideas come out.

## What I'd do differently

Some nights were too loud or too busy, and the people who came hoping for
individual attention didn't always get it. The fix is fewer people per room
and more hosts who know whose work to ask about, not bigger venues.
